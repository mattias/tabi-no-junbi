Chapter one

基礎The basics, for everyone

Do these five steps in order. When you're done, you can review cards every day and add new words with one click.

1

Back up your old AnkiSkip this if you've never used Anki

  1. Open Anki.
  2. Click File›Export…
  3. Choose Anki Collection Package (.colpkg) and tick Include media.
  4. Save the file somewhere safe, such as your Desktop.
2

Install the right Anki versionThe JSchoreels FSRS-7 version of Anki

I use a special version of Anki. It schedules your cards better, and several add-ons below only work with it. It uses the same cards and the same AnkiWeb account as normal Anki.

  1. Close Anki if it's open.
  2. Open the download page:
    Anki download page
  3. Scroll down to Assets and click the file that ends in -win-x64.msi.
    On a Mac with an M chip, take -mac-apple.dmg. On an older Mac, take -mac-intel.dmg.
  4. Double-click the downloaded file and click Next until it's installed.
  5. Open Anki. Your decks are still there.

Tested with version 26.09.3+fsrs7, builds 109 and 110. If a newer build gives you trouble, pick build 110 from the list of all versions.

3

Install the add-onsEleven add-ons in one go

  1. In Anki, click Tools›Add-ons›Get Add-ons…
  2. Click Copy below, then paste into the Code box with Ctrl + V:
    1635024181 218829258 160848019 1798968356 1542952656 1339555413 408592650 1184164376 1771074083 148002038 1151815987
  3. Click OK and wait until it's done.
  4. Close Anki and open it again.

Don't install the normal AnkiConnect (2055492159). It doesn't work with this Anki version. AnkiConnect Extended, which is in the list above, replaces it.

4

Turn on FSRS and add the Kiku card design

Turn on FSRS

  1. Next to your deck, click the ⚙ cog, then Options.
  2. Scroll to FSRS, turn it on and choose FSRS-7. Click Save.
  3. Once you have a few hundred reviews, come back and click Optimize. Do this about once a month.
Remember common words better (optional)

With the Dynamic Desired Retention add-on from step 3, common words come back more often than rare ones, so your reviews go where they pay off most.

  1. In Anki, open Tools›FSRS Dynamic Desired Retention Table.
  2. Under Field Rules, add a rule: note type Kiku, field FreqSort, scope deck:Mining, exclusion is:suspended.
  3. Choose relative values (Top %), Higher DR for lower values, and turn on the smooth steps between rows.
  4. Fill in the rows and click Save:
    WordsDesired retention
    Top 10% (most common)90
    Top 20%80
    Top 33%73
    Top 50%65
    Minimum Retention (the rest)55
  5. The table shows how many reviews per day each row costs. Too many? Lower the bottom rows first.

Cards without a FreqSort value keep the deck's own retention. The rules sync with your collection, and reach kuma3 Anki too if your version supports them.

Add Kiku

  1. Open the download page:
    Kiku download page
  2. Under Assets, click the file named like Kiku_v….apkg.
  3. Double-click the downloaded file. Anki imports it, and you now have a card type called Kiku.
  4. On Anki's main screen, click Create Deck at the bottom and name it Mining. New cards go here.

One Kiku card can test you on a word, a sentence or listening. I make most of mine listening cards, because my goal is to speak. To make a card a listening card, type x in its IsAudioCard field. More help is in Kiku's own guide.

5

Install the pop-up dictionaryHachidori, for Chrome

Hachidori shows what a word means when you point at it on any web page, and adds it to Anki with one click.

  1. Open Hachidori in the Chrome Web Store and click Add to Chrome:
    Hachidori in the Chrome Web Store
    Project page: github.com/bee-san/hachidori. The store version can be a little behind. The newest version is on the download page, with install steps for Chrome and Firefox.
  2. Open its settings: click the puzzle-piece icon at the top right of Chrome, then Hachidori.
  3. Add a dictionary under Dictionaries. Jitendex is a good one: download its ZIP file and import it without unzipping it. A frequency list and a pitch accent dictionary are nice extras.
  4. Under Anki, choose:
    1. Deck: Mining
    2. Note type: Kiku
    3. Match the fields. Kiku's names match what Hachidori suggests.
  5. Under Settings›Sharing, turn sharing on and click Download the Anki add-on. Double-click the downloaded file to install Hachidori Relay in Anki, then restart Anki.

Try it: open any Japanese web page, hold Shift and point at a word. Click + in the pop-up, then check that a new card shows up in your Mining deck.

Anki has to be open while you add cards. Otherwise there's nowhere to send them.

YouTube too: turn on Settings›Advanced›Experimental features›YouTube mining and reload YouTube. Then a card from a caption gets the whole line, its audio and a picture from the video, just like on Netflix.

基礎、完了

That's the basics. Do your reviews every day and add new words with Hachidori. Everything below is optional.

Want to review on your phone too? See kuma3 Anki for Android.

道具 optionalExtra tools: pick what you like

携kuma3 Anki: review on your Android phoneA phone version of the same Anki, so you can carry on where you left off

kuma3 Anki is AnkiDroid built on the same FSRS-7 version of Anki as in chapter one. Your reviews are scheduled the same way on the phone as on your computer. It also has a review heatmap and the time you studied today on each deck.

Android only. It works on almost every phone from the last few years. There's no iPhone version.

On your computer first

  1. In Anki, click Sync at the top, so AnkiWeb has all your latest reviews.

On your phone

  1. Open the download page on your phone:
    kuma3 Anki download page
  2. Under Assets, tap kuma3-anki-arm64.apk.
  3. Open the downloaded file. If the phone asks, allow your browser to install unknown apps, then tap Install.
  4. Open kuma3 Anki and tap the sync button. Sign in with your AnkiWeb account.
  5. When it asks, choose Download. This copies all your cards from AnkiWeb to the phone.

Every day

Tap Sync before and after you review, on both the phone and the computer. Then you always carry on where you left off.

kuma3 Anki installs next to normal AnkiDroid and keeps its own cards. If you also have AnkiDroid, use only one of them for reviews so you don't get two different histories.

Using RWKV on your computer? (That's an extra option in Deck Options for the FSRS-7 version of Anki.) Make the phone use the same model: in kuma3 Anki, open Settings›kuma3 and choose Small for the model and int4 cards, int8 notes for the state precision. Otherwise the two can pick slightly different cards.

Great for cards from books and manga, which have no audio of their own.

You need about 10 GB of free space. An NVIDIA graphics card is recommended. It also works without one, just slowly.

Install

  1. In Anki, click Tools›Add-ons›Get Add-ons… and paste this code:
    2078760911
  2. Restart Anki. A welcome window asks to set up the voice server. Click Install and start. This only happens once and takes 5–30 minutes.

Use

  2. Listen to the result. If you like it, do more cards.
  3. Changed your mind? Edit›Undo removes the whole run.

The voice server stops by itself when you haven't used it for a while, so your graphics card's memory is free for other things.

聴Listening Practice: train your ear on AndroidLoops sentences where you already know every word

It plays only sentences made of words you know, over and over, and hides the text until you ask. You train your ear, not your reading. Great on a walk or on the bus.

Install

  1. Open the download page on your phone and tap the .apk file under Assets:
    Listening Practice download page
  2. Open it. If the phone asks, allow your browser to install unknown apps. It needs Android 8 or newer.

Add your cards

  1. In kuma3 Anki or AnkiDroid, export your Mining deck as .apkg with Include media ticked.
  2. Import the file in Listening Practice and choose the Kiku preset. Every card with a sentence and sentence audio is used.

You can also add your own videos with subtitles, or a YouTube video with captions. Each line becomes a sentence.

Play patterns: instead of a fixed number of plays, set steps like 3 plays at 0.85×, then 3 at 0.90×, then 5 at normal speed. The speed goes up as your ear gets used to the sentence.

The app updates itself, and Immersion Dashboard counts your listening time.

順Surasura: which words to learn nextA word list made from your own books, anime and videos

Surasura reads what you plan to read and watch, and checks which words you already know. Then it lists the words you're missing, with the ones that help you most first. You don't waste time on words you'll never see. It's free, works offline, and does Japanese and Chinese.

Since version 3.0 it also makes your cards, with the line, its audio and a picture, and keeps Anki's new cards in the order you'll meet the words.

1. Install it

  1. On the download page, under Assets, click Surasura-…-setup.exe. You don't need the other files.
    Surasura download page
  2. Double-click it to install. If Windows warns you, click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Have an older Surasura? The same installer brings your known words, word lists and settings over.

2. Set it up

Keep Anki open. Setup asks a few questions:

  1. Which words you already know: choose From Anki. It stays in sync with your cards. It can also read lists from Migaku or Jiten.
  2. What you'll watch and read: choose the folder with your books (EPUB), subtitles and audiobooks, or add a YouTube link. Nothing is moved.
  3. Where each one goes: one key per title. Current is what you're on now, Soon comes next, and Goal is for one day.
  4. Say yes to keeping Anki's new cards in your order (順), and to cards that make themselves. Setup gets Anki Miner for you. You never need to open its window.

Setup offers to install AnkiConnect. Skip that: you already have AnkiConnect Extended from chapter one, and the normal one doesn't work with this Anki version.

In your Mining deck's Options, set New card sort order to Order gathered. Otherwise Anki doesn't keep Surasura's order.

3. Every day

  1. New episodes, books and videos in your folder show up by themselves in New arrivals. Drag them into your order.
  2. Click Mine on an episode. Its cards are made and come next in Anki. Your word list and Anki's order update by themselves.
  3. Watched an episode? Tick its stone, and its words leave your list.

Curious which words are coming? Journey ↗ shows each episode's new words, each with a sentence from your own library. You don't need it day to day.

No text file for a game? Download its deck from Jiten and add it with + Add. Surasura reads Anki deck files (.apkg) too.

Piyo ぴよ lets you practise with your own AI app, on your own words: a short story, a chat or a quiz. Turn it on under Settings›Connections›Piyo ぴよ. The AI company then sees your words and lines from your books and shows.

Stuck? Surasura has its own quick guide and questions and answers. Chapter five shows how I use it every day.

Chapter three

引っ越しMoving from Migaku

Do chapter one first. Your Migaku cards keep all their review history. You give them the Kiku design, then fill in what's missing.

1

Get the cards out of MigakuWith Migaku-Exporter, a small script that runs on Migaku's website

Migaku has no normal way to export to Anki, so we use the free Migaku-Exporter. It brings your cards, pictures, audio and review history over to Anki.

Install the script

  1. Install Tampermonkey from the Chrome Web Store. It lets small scripts run on websites.
  2. Let it run scripts:
    1. Paste this into Chrome's address bar and press Enter:
      chrome://extensions
    2. Under Tampermonkey, click Details and turn on Allow User Scripts. (If you don't see it, turn on Developer mode at the top right instead.)
  3. Open the script: Migaku-Exporter script
    A page full of code opens. Press Ctrl + A, then Ctrl + C to copy all of it.
  4. Click the Tampermonkey icon (under the puzzle piece at the top right of Chrome), then Create a new script.
  5. In the editor that opens, press Ctrl + A and Delete to remove what's there. Then press Ctrl + V to paste, and Ctrl + S to save.

Export your decks

  1. Go to study.migaku.com, sign in, open the Memory page and wait until it has fully loaded.
  2. Click the + button at the bottom right, then Export to Anki.
  3. Pick your decks from the list. Leave the quality on the normal preset.
  4. Click Export selected decks and wait for the progress bar. Decks with many pictures and audio files can take a while.
  5. You get a file called Migaku - deck name.apkg in your Downloads folder. Double-click it to import it into Anki.
2

Change them to Kiku

Make a backup first, as in chapter one, step 1.

  1. Click Browse.
  2. In the list on the left, under Note Types, click the note type that came with the export. It has the same name as your card type in Migaku.
  3. Press Ctrl + A to select all of them.
  4. Click Notes›Change Note Type… and choose Kiku.
  5. Match the fields. The left side is Migaku, the right side is Kiku:
    Migaku fieldKiku field
    WordExpression
    SentenceSentence
    Translated SentenceSentenceTranslation
    DefinitionsMainDefinition
    Word AudioExpressionAudio
    Sentence AudioSentenceAudio
    ImagesPicture
    Example Sentences, Notes(nothing)
  6. Click OK.

If you changed the field names when exporting, pick whatever looks closest. If you leave a field out, it just isn't copied. If you have more than one Migaku card type, repeat these steps for each one.

Your review history came along, so FSRS can learn from it right away. Open your deck's Options and click Optimize, as in chapter one, step 4.

3

Fill in what's missingReadings, frequency, pitch accent and definitions

The backfill add-on from chapter one looks the words up in your Hachidori dictionaries and fills in the empty fields.

  1. Make sure Anki is open, Chrome is open, and Hachidori's Settings›Sharing is on.
  2. In Anki, click Browse and select 10 cards to test with first.
  3. Click Edit›Backfill from Yomitan.
  4. Under Preset, choose lapis.json (it works for Kiku too), then click Run.
  5. Look at the 10 cards. If they look right, select all your cards and do it again.

If it says it can't connect, restart Anki, open Chrome, and try again. Hachidori Relay gives the add-on the dictionaries while Anki is running.

Chapter five

旅の流れHow it all fits together

This is how I use the tools day to day. The idea: learn a book's or show's new words before you start it. Then you can just enjoy it, without looking words up or stopping to make cards.

1

Find the words to learn nextWith Surasura

Put the book or the show's subtitles in your Surasura folder before you start reading or watching, and drag it into your order. For a game, or anything else without a text file, use its deck from Jiten (see Surasura). Surasura knows which words you have in Anki, so it lists the words you're missing, the most useful to you first. You never need to finish the list.

The Level setting decides how deep the list goes: only the common words, or rarer ones too. Automatic picks it for you.

2

Let Surasura make the cardsWith Mine, which uses Anki Miner

Click Mine on the episode or chapter. Each word gets a card cut from its own line, with the sentence, audio and a picture. The new cards go straight into Anki in the order you'll meet the words, and if you move a show, Anki's order follows. Anki has to be open.

3

Add the rest by handWith Nadeshiko

Some words don't get a card, for example when no line has clean audio. Search for them on Nadeshiko, which finds real sentences from anime and dramas. It has its own Anki settings, so you set it up once.

  1. Search for the word on Nadeshiko.
  2. Pick a good sentence and add the word with Hachidori, just like on any web page. This makes the new card.
  3. Under the same sentence, click Add, then Add to Anki (Last added card). Nadeshiko puts the audio and the picture on the card you just made.

Keep the sentences short. They're easier to understand by ear and easier to say out loud.

4

Review in Anki, and listenFSRS decides when each card comes back

Do your reviews every day. On a listening card, play the audio two or three times and say the sentence out loud before you turn it over.

On the go, Listening Practice loops sentences from your cards where you already know every word.

5

Read, watch and listenThe fun part, and the most important one

  • Read books in Hoshi Reader.
  • Watch anime in mpv with hachidori-mpv.
  • Play games and visual novels with GameSentenceMiner running, so you can look words up as you play.
  • Watch YouTube, and listen to podcasts and music.

You already know most of the new words, so you don't need to look much up, and you don't stop to make cards. That keeps you in the story.

Before the next book or show, add it to Surasura and go back to step 1.

Immersion Dashboard shows how much you read, watch and review, so you can see your progress.
